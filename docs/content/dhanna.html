33  The mental verbs

33.1 Introduction

Consider the sentence:

  1. زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ
    « Zayd is standing. »

This entire sentence can be the inner sentence of a compound sentence as the direct object of the verb قَالَ. So we can say:

  1. قُلْتُ زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ
    « I said, “Zayd is standing.” »

In example () above, the verb قَالَ does not exercise its government within the inner sentence زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ. Rather, the inner sentence is, as a whole, the (positional) direct object. This is because the verb قَالَ does not have a meaningful connection with the content of the inner sentence. It is simply relating the sentence as direct speech.

In addition to a nounal sentence like زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ, a verbal sentence too can be the direct object of قَالَ. So, for example, we can say:

  1. قُلْتُ ضَرَبَ زَيْدٌ [شرح الرضي على الكافية]
    « I said, “Zayd beat.” »

Now let’s consider what happens when a nounal sentence is made the direct object of a class of verbs called mental verbs.

Mental verbs are a class of verbs that signify actions taking place within the mind. Some examples of mental verbs are:

  • ظَنَّ « to conjecture, assume »
  • عَلِمَ « to know »
  • حَسِبَ « to deem »
  • خَالَ « to imagine »

These verbs can take a inner nounal sentence as a direct object, but instead of taking the entire inner sentence as one direct object, the mental verb exercises its government within the inner sentence and governs the original subject and info of the inner sentence individually as two direct objects. For example:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا
    « I assumed Zayd to be standing. »

Note that زَيْدًا and قَائِمًا are both in the a-case because they are, individually, the direct objects of ظَنَّ. The verb ظَنَّ is able to break up the original nounal sentence زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ and govern its constituents individually because it has a meaningful connection with the content of the inner sentence. This is because the original subject (زَيْد)’s relationship with the original info (قَائِم) is only established via the meaning of the verb ظَنَّ. That is, the sentence in example () above is not saying that Zayd is standing. Rather it is saying that I thought Zayd to be standing. So Zayd’s “standing-ness” is only established via my assumption of it.

The mental verb thus describes the nature of speaker’s belief of the actuality of the original info ocurring from the original subject.1

So, for example, ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا signifies my assumption of Zayd’s standing. And عَلِمْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا signifies my knowledge of Zayd’s standing.

33.2 The lean of the mental verb sentence

The basic nounal sentence consists of a subject and a info. For example:

  1. زَيْدٌ أَخُوكَ
    « Zayd is your brother. »

In example () above, زَيْدٌ is the subject and أَخُوكَ is its info. Both are in the u-case because it is a basic nounal sentence. In terms of its lean, زَيْدٌ is the leaned-against element and أَخُوكَ is the leaning element. (Technically, the leaning element is أَخ. And it in turn is a annexe to the annexed-to كَ. But loosely speaking, we can say that the leaning element is the bundled أَخُوكَ.) With the leaned-against element and the leaning element in place, the sentence is leaned.

Now consider the following sentence:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا أَخَاكَ [Peled, Sentence types 188]
    « I thought Zayd to be your brother. »

In example () above, the verb ظَنَّ takes the subject and info of example (), and makes them into direct objects. The verb ظَنَّ requires a doer (as usual), which is the pronoun تُ « I ». And it also requires two direct objects: زَيْدًا is the first direct object and أَخَاكَ is the second direct object.

This sentence is peculiar in its lean. This is because the indispensable elements are the two direct objects. زَيْدًا is the leaned-against element and أَخَاكَ is the leaning element.2 The sentence is only leaned when both direct objects are in place. The sentence may still be categorized as a verbal sentence. However, unlike normal verbal sentences, the verb ظَنَّ and its doer are not the indispensable elements of the sentence. Rather ظَنَّ is the foundational government. Figure () depicts the government of example ().

Parallels of the lean of the ظَنَّ sentence may be found in the كَانَ and إِنَّ sentences:

  1. كَانَ زَيْدٌ أَخَاكَ [Peled, Sentence types 188]
    « Zayd was your brother. »
    (زَيْدٌ, the doer/subject, is the leaned-against element. أَخَاكَ, the direct object/info, is the leaning element. كَانَ is the foundational government.)

  2. إِنَّ زَيْدًا أَخُوكَ [Peled, Sentence types 188]
    « Indeed Zayd is your brother. »
    (زَيْدًا, the subject, is the leaned-against element. أَخُوكَ, the info, is the leaning element. إِنَّ is the foundational government.)

33.3 The mental verbs as passive verbs

When mental verbs are used in the passive voice, the first direct object (the one which is the subject in the basic nounal sentence) is made the deputy doer in the u-case. And the second direct object (the one which is the info in the basic nounal sentence) remains a direct object in the a-case. So, example () above, becomes in the passive voice:

  1. ظُنَّ زَيْدٌ أَخَاكَ
    « Zayd was thought to be your brother. »

Similarly,

  1. زَيْدٌ مَظْنُونٌ شُجَاعًا [Wright 2/52D]
    « Zayd is thought to be brave. »

33.4 أَنَّ and أَنْ with mental verbs

Consider again the sentence:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا
    « I assumed Zayd to be standing. »

Literally زَيْدًا and قَائِمًا are both, individually, the direct objects of ظَنَّ. However, in terms of meaning, the taqdīr of the sentence is:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ قِيَامَ زَيْدٍ
    « I assumed Zayd to be standing. »

That is, in the taqdīr of the sentence, ظَنَّ has one direct object which is the maṣdar (قِيَام) of the second direct object (قَائِم) annexed to the first direct object (زَيْد).

We have seen maṣdars as a taqdīr before in the analysis of أَنَّ and أَنْ. And this similarity allows أَنَّ and أَنْ (with their clauses) to optionally occur as the direct object of mental verbs.3 So instead of saying ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا, we can instead say:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ أَنَّ زَيْدًا قَائِمٌ
    « I assumed that Zayd is standing. »

or:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ أَنْ يَقُومَ زَيْدٌ
    « I assumed that Zayd would stand. »

Now the أَنَّ and أَنْ clauses, respectively, are, formally, the single direct object of ظَنَّ. This substitution of the two direct objects of mental verbs can be considered a form of suspension,4 but is really more basic than it. We will study suspension in Section 33.10 below, if Allāh wills.

In terms of meaning, example () above is more assertive than the original example (). Whereas, example () is less assertive and more doubtful than the original example ().

Here is another example of substituting the direct objects of a mental verb with an أَنَّ clause:

  1. عَلِمْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا
    « I knew Zayd to be standing. »

  2. عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ زَيْدًا قَائِمٌ
    « I knew that Zayd is standing. »

In this case, we will only use أَنَّ and not أَنْ because, as we learned in section (), only أَنَّ is used in cases of certainty, which is what the verb عَلِمَ implies.

In examples () and (), it was easy to come up with a maṣdari equivalence of the two direct objects of the mental verbs. This is because the second direct object (قَائِم) was a deverbal noun. But what if the the second direct object was a primitive noun? For example:

  1. عَلِمْتُ أَخَاكَ زَيْدًا [شرح الرضي على الكافية 3/152]
    « I knew your brother to be Zayd. »

In this case we can manufacture a maṣdar from the primitive noun, and still retain the taqdīr, thus:

  1. عَلِمْتُ زَيْدِيَّةَ أَخِيكَ [شرح الرضي على الكافية 3/152]
    « I knew the Zayd-ness of your brother. »

And using this maṣdari equivalence, we can substitute it with أَنَّ, thus:

  1. عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ أَخَاكَ زَيْدٌ
    « I knew that your brother is Zayd. »

33.5 The verbs that comprise the mental verbs

33.5.1 ظَنَّ

ظَنَّ يَظُنُّ is the typical mental verb. Its basic meaning is « to conjecture » or « to assume ». But it (and the mental verbs in general) can often be translated as « to think ».

ظَنَّ can be used in a range of circumstances from situations where its doer believes something to be certain, to cases where he is doubtful. Here are some examples:

  1. وَإِنِّی لَأَظُنُّكَ یَـٰفِرۡعَوۡنُ مَثۡبُورࣰا [سورة الإسراء 17:102 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/331]
    « and indeed I think, O Pharaoh, that you are destroyed. »

  2. قُلۡتُم مَّا نَدۡرِی مَا ٱلسَّاعَةُ إِن نَّظُنُّ إِلَّا ظَنࣰّا وَمَا نَحۡنُ بِمُسۡتَیۡقِنِینَ [سورة الجاثية 45:32 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/331]
    « you said, ‘We know not what is the Hour. We assume only assumption, and we are not convinced.’ »

  3. ظَنَنْتُكَ إِنْ شَبَّتْ لَظَى الْحَرْبِ صَالِيًا [cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/331]
    « I thought you, if the blaze of war is kindled, to be entering (therein) »

ظَنَّ following examples is interpreted to have certainty in its meaning:

  1. قَالَ ٱلَّذِینَ یَظُنُّونَ أَنَّهُم مُّلَـٰقُوا۟ ٱللَّهِ كَم مِّن فِئَةࣲ قَلِیلَةٍ غَلَبَتۡ فِئَةࣰ كَثِیرَةَۢ بِإِذۡنِ ٱللَّهِۗ وَٱللَّهُ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِینَ5 [سورة البقرة 2:249 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/332]
    « But those who were certain that they would meet Allāh said, “How many a small company has overcome a large company by permission of Allāh. And Allāh is with the patient.” »

  2. إِنِّی ظَنَنتُ أَنِّی مُلَـٰقٍ حِسَابِیَهۡ [سورة الحاقة 69:20 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/332]
    « Indeed, I was certain that I would be meeting my account.” »

  3. وَرَءَا ٱلۡمُجۡرِمُونَ ٱلنَّارَ فَظَنُّوۤا۟ أَنَّهُم مُّوَاقِعُوهَا وَلَمۡ یَجِدُوا۟ عَنۡهَا مَصۡرِفࣰا6 [سورة الكهف 18:53 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/332]
    « And the criminals will see the Fire and will be certain that they are to fall therein. And they will not find from it a way elsewhere. »

ظَنَّ can be used with both أَنَّ (as above) and the a-case-izing أَنْ. Here is an example with the a-case-izing أَنْ:

  1. تَظُنُّ أَن یُفۡعَلَ بِهَا فَاقِرَةࣱ [سورة القيامة 75:25]

Even so, example () above is interpreted to have certitude, or at least a preponderance of belief, in its meaning.7

The lightened أَنْ can also be used. For example:

  1. وَظَنُّوۤا۟ أَن لَّا مَلۡجَأَ مِنَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَّاۤ إِلَیۡهِ [سورة التوبة 9:118]

أَنْ in example () above is interpreted to be the lightened أَنْ.8

Sometimes ظَنَّ can have a specialized meaning « to suspect ». In this case, it no longer needs two direct objects, and will take only one direct object. For example:

  1. ظَنَّ القَاضِي فُلَانًا [النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/331]
    « The judge suspected so-and-so. »

  2. عُدِمَ لِي مَالٌ فَظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا [Howell §442 2/150]
    « Some property of mine was missing so I suspected Zayd. »

33.5.2 عَلِمَ

عَلِمَ يَعْلَمُ means « to know ». Here are some examples of its usage:

  1. عَلِمْتُكَ الْبَاذِلَ الْمَعْرُوفَ [Wright 2/49B]
    « I have known you to be liberal in the exercise of bounty. »

  2. فَإِنۡ عَلِمۡتُمُوهُنَّ مُؤۡمِنَـٰتࣲ فَلَا تَرۡجِعُوهُنَّ إِلَى ٱلۡكُفَّارِۖ [سورة الممتحنة 60:10 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/325]
    « And if you know them to be believers, then do not return them to the disbelievers; »

  3. عَنِ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ، وَالْحَسَنِ بْنِ عَلِيٍّ ، مَرَّتْ بِهِمَا جَنَازَةٌ فَقَامَ أَحَدُهُمَا وَقَعَدَ الآخَرُ فَقَالَ الَّذِي قَامَ أَمَا وَاللَّهِ لَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَدْ قَامَ قَالَ لَهُ الَّذِي جَلَسَ لَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم قَدْ جَلَسَ [سنن النسائي :1926]
    « [It was narrated] from Ibn Ɛabbās and al‑Ḥasan ibn Ɛaliyy: A funeral passed by them and one of them stood and the other [stayed] seated. The one who stood up said, “By Allāh, I know that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had stood up.” The one who [stayed] seated said to him, “Indeed I know that the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ had [stayed] seated.” »

  4. إِنَّ رَبَّكَ یَعۡلَمُ أَنَّكَ تَقُومُ أَدۡنَىٰ مِن ثُلُثَیِ ٱلَّیۡلِ وَنِصۡفَهُۥ وَثُلُثَهُۥ وَطَاۤىِٕفَةࣱ مِّنَ ٱلَّذِینَ مَعَكَۚ وَٱللَّهُ یُقَدِّرُ ٱلَّیۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَۚ عَلِمَ أَن لَّن تُحۡصُوهُ فَتَابَ عَلَیۡكُمۡۖ فَٱقۡرَءُوا۟ مَا تَیَسَّرَ مِنَ ٱلۡقُرۡءَانِۚ عَلِمَ أَن سَیَكُونُ مِنكُم مَّرۡضَىٰ وَءَاخَرُونَ یَضۡرِبُونَ فِی ٱلۡأَرۡضِ یَبۡتَغُونَ مِن فَضۡلِ ٱللَّهِ وَءَاخَرُونَ یُقَـٰتِلُونَ فِی سَبِیلِ ٱللَّهِۖ فَٱقۡرَءُوا۟ مَا تَیَسَّرَ مِنۡهُۚ وَأَقِیمُوا۟ ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ وَءَاتُوا۟ ٱلزَّكَوٰةَ وَأَقۡرِضُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ قَرۡضًا حَسَنࣰاۚ وَمَا تُقَدِّمُوا۟ لِأَنفُسِكُم مِّنۡ خَیۡرࣲ تَجِدُوهُ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ هُوَ خَیۡرࣰا وَأَعۡظَمَ أَجۡرࣰاۚ وَٱسۡتَغۡفِرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَۖ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورࣱ رَّحِیمُۢ [سورة المزمل 73:20]
    « Indeed, your Lord knows, [O Muḥammad], that you stand [in prayer] almost two thirds of the night or half of it or a third of it, and [so do] a group of those with you. And Allāh determines [the extent of] the night and the day. He has known that you [Muslims] will not be able to do it and has turned to you in forgiveness, so recite what is easy [for you] of the Qur’ān. He has known that there will be among you those who are ill and others traveling throughout the land seeking [something] of the bounty of Allāh and others fighting for the cause of Allāh. So recite what is easy from it and establish prayer and give zakāh and loan Allāh a goodly loan. And whatever good you put forward for yourselves - you will find it with Allāh. It is better and greater in reward. And seek forgiveness of Allāh. Indeed, Allāh is Forgiving and Merciful. »
    (أَنْ in أَنْ لَنْ and أَنْ سيكونُ is the lightened أَنْ,9 whose subject is a deleted state-of-affairs pronoun.10)

33.5.3 رَأَى

رَأَى يَرَى, as a sensory verb, means « to see » with the eye. As such, it will typically take one direct object. For example:

  1. رَأَيْتُهُ [Howell §442 2/150]
    « I saw him »

When رَأَىٰ has this sensory meaning, then there may be a second a-case (indefinite) governee as a ḥāl.11 For example:

  1. رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا ضَاحِكًا [البسيط لابن أبي الربيع 445]
    « I saw Zayd laughing. »

رَأَىٰ can also mean « to hold (an opinion) ». In this case as well, it will take one direct object. For example:

  1. رَأَىٰ أَبُو حَنِيفَةَ حِلَّ كَذَا [Howell §442 2/150]
    « Abū Ḥanīfah held (the opinion of) the lawfulness of such a thing. »

As a mental verb, رَأَى means « to regard » in the mind. It may still be translated as « to see », but this “seeing” is with the mind. Now رَأَى will take take two direct objects.

Here are some examples of رَأَى:

  1. رَأَيْتُ اللهَ أَكْبَرَ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ مُحَاوَلَةً وَأَكْثَرُهُمْ جُنُودًا [Howell §440 2/134]
    « I regarded Allāh as the greatest of eveything in resource, and the most numerous of them in hosts. »

  2. إِنَّهُمۡ یَرَوۡنَهُۥ بَعِیدࣰا۝ وَنَرَىٰهُ قَرِیبࣰا۝ [سورة المعارج 70:6–7]
    « Indeed, they see it [as] distant, But We see it [as] near. »

Even when رَأَى contains the meaning of a sensory verb, it can still take two direct objects when the mind takes what it sees and regards it to be something.12 For example:

  1. يَا مَعْشَرَ النِّسَاءِ تَصَدَّقْنَ وَأَكْثِرْنَ فَإِنِّي رَأَيْتُكُنَّ أَكْثَرَ أَهْلِ النَّارِ لِكَثْرَةِ اللَّعْنِ وَكُفْرِ الْعَشِيرِ [مسند أحمد :5343 cited by البسيط لابن أبي الربيع 447 footnote 1]
    « O women, give charity and do a lot of it, for indeed I saw you to be the majority of the people of the Fire, because of much cursing [on your part] and being ungrateful to the husband. »

  2. رَأَیۡتُهُمۡ لِی سَـٰجِدِینَ [سورة يوسف 12:4 cited by شرح الرضي على الكافية 4/151]
    « I saw them prostrating to me. »

33.5.4 حَسِبَ

حَسِبَ يَحْسَِبُ means « to deem ». As with ظَنَّ, it can be translated with the verb « think ». Here are some examples:

  1. حَسِبْتُ التُّقَىٰ وَالْجُودَ خَيْرَ تِجَارَةٍ رَبَاحًا [Wright 2/49C]
    « I deemed piety and generosity the best merchandise, in terms of profit. »

  2. وَلَا تَحۡسَبَنَّ ٱلَّذِینَ قُتِلُوا۟ فِی سَبِیلِ ٱللَّهِ أَمۡوَ ٰ⁠تَۢاۚ [سورة آل عمران 3:169 cited by Wright 2/49C]
    « And never think of those who have been killed in the cause of Allāh as dead. »

  3. صَنَعْنَا لِرَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ فَخَّارَةً ، فَأَتَيْتُهُ بِهَا ، فَوَضَعْتُهَا بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ ، فَاطَّلَعَ فِيهَا ، فَقَالَ : حَسِبْتُهُ لَحْمًا [مسند أحمد :14581]
    « We made a (clay) pot (of food) for the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ and I brought it to him. He peered into it and said, “I thought it was meat.” »

  4. أَحَسِبَ ٱلنَّاسُ أَن یُتۡرَكُوۤا۟ أَن یَقُولُوۤا۟ ءَامَنَّا وَهُمۡ لَا یُفۡتَنُونَ [سورة العنكبوت 29:2 cited by شرح ألفية ابن مالك للحازمي 43/2]
    « Do the people think that they will be left to say, “We believe” and they will not be tried? »
    (The maṣdar from أَنْ يُتْرَكُوا fulfils the role of the two direct objects.13)

33.5.5 وَجَدَ

وَجَدَ يَجِدُ is also a sensory verb which means « to find ». When it is used to mean that the mind has found something to be another thing, then it is used as a mental verb and can take two direct objects. Examples:

  1. وَإِن وَجَدۡنَاۤ أَكۡثَرَهُمۡ لَفَـٰسِقِینَ [سورة الأعراف 7:102 cited by شرح ابن عقيل على الألفية 2/31]
    « but indeed, We found most of them defiantly disobedient. »
    (أَكْثَرَهُمْ and لَفَاسِقِينَ are both analyzed to be direct objects.)

  2. كُنَّا مَعْشَرُ قُرَيْشٍ قَوْمًا نَغْلِبُ النِّسَاءَ فَلَمَّا قَدِمْنَا الْمَدِينَةَ وَجَدْنَاهُمْ قَوْمًا تَغْلِبُهُمْ نِسَاؤُهُمْ [صحيح ابن حبان :4268a]
    « We, the people of Qurays͡h, were a people who were dominant over the women. But when we came to al‑Madīnah, we found them to be a people whose women dominated them. »

As with رَأَى, when وَجَدَ is used merely as a sensory verb, then it will take only one direct object. A second a-case noun with it will then be construed as a ḥāl.14 For example:

  1. وَجَدْتُهُ مَرِيضًا [Wright 2/50C]
    « I found him sick. »
    (مَرِيضًا is a ḥāl.)

Similarly:

  1. أَلَمۡ یَجِدۡكَ یَتِیمࣰا فَـَٔاوَىٰ۝ وَوَجَدَكَ ضَاۤلࣰّا فَهَدَىٰ۝ وَوَجَدَكَ عَاۤىِٕلࣰا فَأَغۡنَىٰ۝ [سورة الضحى 93:6–8]
    « Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge? And He found you lost and guided [you], And He found you poor and made [you] self-sufficient. »
    (يَتِيمًا, ضَالًّا, and عَائِلًا are analyzed as ḥāls.15 Although, a direct object interpretation for at least one of them also seems to be possible.16)

33.5.6 خَالَ

خَالَ يَخَالُ خِلْتُ means « to imagine or fancy (something) ». It too is often translated as « to think ». خَالَ is irregular in that its speaker participant singular for the pc verb (« I imagine ») is more commonly إِخَالُ ʾik͡hālu instead of أَخَالُ ʾak͡hālu. For example,

  1. إِخَالُ زَيْدًا أَخَاكَ [Wright 2/49C]
    « I imagine Zayd to be your brother. »

Here are some examples of خَالَ as a mental verb:

  1. فَأُتِيَ بِكُرْسِيٍّ خِلْتُ قَوَائِمَهُ حَدِيدًا [سنن النسائي :5377] « Then he was brought a chair, whose legs I imagined to be of iron. »

  2. مَا إِخَالُ أَحَدًا يَعْقِلُ يَرْمِي حَتَّى تَطْلُعَ الشَّمْسُ [مسند أحمد :2082]
    « I don’t imagine anyone of sense to stone until the sun rises. »

    (The first direct object is أَحَدًا. And the second direct object is the verbal sentence يَرْمِي.)

33.5.11 سَمِعَ

سَمِعَ is, by default, a sensory verb that means « to hear ». And, as such, it takes one direct object. For example:

  1. سَمِعْتُ زَيْدًا
    « I heard Zayd. »

  2. سَمِعْتُ صَوْتًا
    « I heard a sound. »

But, as a special case, سَمِعَ can take two direct objects when the doer hears someone to be doing something. For example:

  1. سَمِعْتُ زَيْدٌ قَارِئًا [البسيط لابن أبي الربيع 446]
    « I heard Zayd reading. »
    (The second direct object is the noun قَارِئًا.)

  2. سَمِعْتُ زَيْدٌ يَقْرَأُ [Howell §445 2/165]
    « I heard Zayd reading. »
    (The second direct object is the verbal sentence يَقْرَأُ.)

In these examples, قَارِئًا and يَقْرَأُ are not considered a ḥāl (as ضَاحِكًا was considered a ḥāl in example () رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا ضَاحِكًا « I saw Zayd laughing. »). This is because the sentence, in this context, would not be leaned if you did not add the second direct object. That is, if, in this context, you said سَمِعْتُ زَيْدٌ and stopped, the listener would be left wondering, “You heard Zayd doing what?” So a second direct object has to be added which gives the action that Zayd was doing which produced a sound.19 Similarly,

  1. قَالُوا۟ سَمِعۡنَا فَتࣰى یَذۡكُرُهُمۡ یُقَالُ لَهُۥۤ إِبۡرَ ٰ⁠هِیمُ [سورة الأنبياء 21:60]
    « They said, “We heard a young man mention them who is called Abraham.” »
    (يَذْكُرُهُمْ is analyzed to be the second direct object of سَمِعَ.20)

33.6 The second direct object can be a sentence or phrase

Because mental verbs govern what was originally a subject-info sentence, the first direct object may be anything which is allowed to be a subject, and the second direct object may be anything which is allowed to be a info. Thus, the second direct object may be a complete sentence in the positional a-case, or an adverbial or prepositional phrase.21

Here are some examples where the second direct object is a complete sentence:

  1. مَا عَلِمْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم نَكَحَ شَيْئًا مِنْ نِسَائِهِ وَلاَ أَنْكَحَ شَيْئًا مِنْ بَنَاتِهِ عَلَى أَكْثَرَ مِنْ ثِنْتَىْ عَشْرَةَ أُوقِيَّةً [جامع الترمذي :1114b]
    « I did not know the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ to marry any of his women, nor to give any of his daughters in marriage, for more than twelve Ūqiyahs. »
    (First direct object is رَسُول الله « the Messenger of Allāh », second direct object is نَكَحَ « he married ».)

  2. مَا عَلِمْتُهُ صَامَ شَهْرًا كُلَّهُ إِلاَّ رَمَضَانَ [صحيح مسلم :1156b]
    « I did not know him to fast any complete month except Ramad̤͡hān. »
    (First direct object is هُ « him », second direct object is صَامَ « he fasted ».)

  3. ظَنَنْتُهُ يَدْعُو فِي الِاسْتِسْقَاءِ [صحيح ابن خزيمة :1595] « I thought him to be supplicating in the rain prayer. »
    (First direct object is هُ « him », second direct object is يَدْعُو « supplicating ».)

Here is an example where the second direct object is a prepositional phrase:

  1. قَالَ أَمَا وَاللَّهِ إِنَّ ذَلِكَ لِتَغَيُّرِ ثَائِرَةٍ كَانَتْ مِنِّي إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَكِنِّي أَرَاهُمْ عَلَى ضَلَالَةٍ [مستدرك الحاكم :4956]
    « He said, “By Allāh, indeed that is because of a change in a matter between me and them, but I think them to be on misguidance.” »
    (First direct object is هُمْ « them », second direct object is عَلَى ضَلَالٍ « on misguidance ».)

33.7 The first direct object may be a state-of-affairs pronoun

Just like the second direct object may be a complete sentence, similarly, the first direct object may be a state-of-affairs pronoun because a state-of-affairs pronoun can regularly be the subject of a nounal sentence. So we can say:

  1. حَسِبْتُ يَقُولُ زَيْدٌ [شرح الرضي على الكافية 3/152]
    « I deemed [the state-of-affairs to be] Zayd is saying. »

In example () above, the first direct object of حَسِبَ is a deleted state-of-affairs pronoun, as if to say: حَسِبْتُهُ يَقُولُ زَيْدٌ.22

However, such usage will be rare.23

33.8 Mental verbs with only one direct object

So far we have focused on mental verbs that take two direct objects. There are other mental verbs that take only one direct object, or no direct object. They are not so much the subject of this chapter, except that they can undertake a phenomenon called suspension that we will study in Section 33.10 below. In any case, we give examples of some of these verbs:

[Examples of عَرَفَ، تَفَكَّرَ]

Some of the mental verbs that can take two direct objects, while keeping their meaning as mental verbs, can take only one direct object. عَلِمَ is the prime example of this variation. For example, we may say:

  1. عَلِمْتُ زَيْدًا [شرح الرضي على الكافية (جامعة الإمام محمد) 2/988]
    « I knew Zayd. »

  2. عَلِمْتُ خُرُوجَ زَيْدٍ [شرح الرضي على الكافية (جامعة الإمام محمد) 2/988]
    « I knew Zayd’s going out. »

Similarly:

  1. وَٱللَّهُ أَخۡرَجَكُم مِّنۢ بُطُونِ أُمَّهَـٰتِكُمۡ لَا تَعۡلَمُونَ شَیۡـࣰٔا [سورة النحل 16:78 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/236]
    « And Allāh has extracted you from the wombs of your mothers not knowing a thing »

  2. لَقَدْ عَلِمْتُ النَّظَائِرَ الَّتِي كَانَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم يَقْرَأُ بِهِنَّ سُورَتَيْنِ فِي رَكْعَةٍ [صحيح مسلم :822e]
    « I know the manners in which the Messenger of Allāh ﷺ recited two sūrahs in one rakɛah. »

Other mental verbs, like ظَنَّ usually require two direct objects.24 Even so, sometimes they may take only one direct object when that direct object refers to the thought being perceived. This is because, as we discussed in section () above, even when the mental verb takes two direct objects, their taqdīr is a single direct object.25 Here is an example of ظَنَّ with one direct object:

  1. وَلَقَدْ نَزَلْتِ فَلَا تَظُنِّي غَيْرَهُ مِنِّي بِمَنْزِلَةً الْمُحَبِّ الْمُكْرَمِ [شرح الرضي على الكافية (جامعة الإمام محمد) 2/988]
    « And surely you have alighted, so don’t assume other than it from me, in the place of alighting of the honored beloved. »

ظَنَّ in example () above has only one direct object غَيْرَهُ. Similarly, the single direct object of ظَنَّ can be a pronoun or pointing noun referring to the the thought being perceived. For example:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ ذَاكَ [كتاب سيبويه 3/120]
    « I thought that. »

In example () above, ذَاكَ can be the single direct object of ظَنَّ, that refers to some thought.26 The reason why ذَاكَ can be a direct object, and not necessarily refer to the maṣdar as a absolute object (as we will see shortly), is because one can add the maṣdar separately as the absolute object. For example:

  1. خِلْتُ ذَاكَ إِخَالُــهُ خَالًا [شرح التسهيل لابن مالك 2/65]
    « I imagined that, imagining it [with] an imagining. »
    (خَالًا is the absolute object while إِخَالُهُ is taking the pronoun هُ as a direct object.)

Similarly, if someone says:

  1. أَظُنُّ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا [شرح الرضي على الكافية]
    « I assume Zayd to be standing. »

then the reply to this can be:

  1. أَنَا أَيْضًا أَظُنُّــهُ [شرح الرضي على الكافية]
    « I too assume it. »
    (The pronoun هُ is a direct object and refers to the thought “Zayd is standing”.)

Having said this, some grammarians were of the opinion that a single pronoun or pointing noun governed by ظَنَّ can only refer to the maṣdar of ظَنَّ, such that it is a deputy to the maṣdar as the absolute object.27

ظَنَّ can also occur with an indirect done-to, which signifies the thing being thought about. For example:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ بِهِ [شرح الرضي على الكافية 4/153]
    « I thought about him. »

Consider also the following āyah:

  1. وَطَاۤىِٕفَةࣱ قَدۡ أَهَمَّتۡهُمۡ أَنفُسُهُمۡ یَظُنُّونَ بِٱللَّهِ غَیۡرَ ٱلۡحَقِّ ظَنَّ ٱلۡجَـٰهِلِیَّةِۖ
    [سورة آل عمران 3:154 cited by شرح الرضي على الكافية 4/153]
    « while another faction worried about themselves, thinking of Allāh other than the truth - the thought of ignorance »

There are a few different analyses of ظَنَّ and its governees:

  1. غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ is the direct object and بِاللهِ is indicating who is being thought about,28 like:
    1. ظَنَنْتُ بِزَيْدٍ [الدر المصون للسمين الحلبي for سورة آل عمران 3:154]
      « I thought about Zayd »
  2. غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ is the first direct object and بِاللهِ is the second direct object.29
  3. غَيْرَ الْحَقِّ is the a deputy to the maṣdar as a absolute object, and ظَنَّ الْجَاهِلِيَّةِ is its substitute.30

33.9 Suppressing one or both direct objects

Non-mental verbs that can take two direct objects may sometimes take only one direct object. So, for example, instead of saying:

  1. أَعْطَيْتُ زَيْدًا دِرْهَمًا
    « I gave Zayd a dirham. »

we can say both:

  1. أَعْطَيْتُ دِرْهَمًا
    [Howell §443 2/152]
    « I gave a dirham. »

  2. أَعْطَيْتُ زَيْدًا
    [Howell §443 2/152]
    « I gave Zayd. »

Mental verbs, on the other hand, generally need both direct objects. So, for example, instead of saying:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا [Howell §443 2/154]
    « I thought Zayd to be standing. »

we cannot say (without context):

  1. ✗ ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا [Howell §443 2/154]

  2. ✗ ظَنَنْتُ قَائِمًا [Howell §443 2/154]

However, it is possible to omit one of the two direct objects if its meaning is implied and is indicated by context.31 For example,

  1. وَلَا یَحۡسَبَنَّ ٱلَّذِینَ یَبۡخَلُونَ بِمَاۤ ءَاتَىٰهُمُ ٱللَّهُ مِن فَضۡلِهِۦ هُوَ خَیۡرࣰا لَّهُمۖ [سورة آل عمران 3:180 cited by Howell §443 2/153–15432]
    « And let not those who [greedily] withhold what Allāh has given them of His bounty ever think that it is better for them. »

In the āyah above, the first direct object of يَحْسَبَنَّ is interpreted (in one interpretation of this reading) to be a suppressed بُخْلَهُمْ « their miserliness », and هُوَ is a separating-pronoun. Such that the meaning is:

  1. ولا يَحْسَبَنَّ الباخِلون بُخْلَهم هو خيرًا لهم [الدر المصون للسمين الحلبي for سورة آل عمران 3:180]
    « And let not the misers think their miserliness to be good for them. »

Both direct objects can also be omitted together. For example:

  1. مَنْ يَسْمَعْ يَخَلْ [Howell §443 2/153]
    « Whoso hears, will think. »

The preposition بِ may be used with ظَنَّ to mean « about ». In this case as well, no direct object is needed. For example:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ بِهِ [Howell §443 2/154]
    « I thought about him. »

Even in such cases where both direct objects are omitted, their meaning may be implied. For example:

  1. وَیَوۡمَ یُنَادِیهِمۡ فَیَقُولُ أَیۡنَ شُرَكَاۤءِیَ ٱلَّذِینَ كُنتُمۡ تَزۡعُمُونَ [سورة القصص 28:62 cited by Howell §443 2/153]
    « And [warn of] the Day He will call them and say, “Where are My ‘partners’ which you used to claim?” »

In the āyah above, the two suppressed direct objects of تَزْعُمُونَ are interpreted to be thus:

  1. تَزْعُمُونَهُمْ شُرَكَاءَ [Howell §443 2/153]
    « … you claim them to be partners »

Suppressing one or both direct objects in the answer to a question is especially common. For example, in response to the question:

  1. أتظن زيدًا قائما [شرح ألفية ابن مالك للشاطبي 2/495]
    « Do you think Zayd to be standing? »

All three of the following replies are acceptable:

  1. ظننت زيدًا [شرح ألفية ابن مالك للشاطبي 2/495]
    « I think Zayd [to be standing]. »

  2. ظننتُ قائمًا [شرح ألفية ابن مالك للشاطبي 2/495]
    « I think [Zayd to be] standing. »

  3. ظننت [شرح ألفية ابن مالك للشاطبي 2/495]
    « I think [Zayd to be standing]. »

Similarly:

  1. قُلْتُ صَلَّاهَا النَّبِيُّ ﷺ قَالَ لَا إِخَالُ [مصنف ابن أبي شيبة :7856]
    « I said, “Did the Prophet ﷺ pray it?” He said, “I don’t think so.” »

33.10 Suspension of government

Sometimes, there can be a sentence where a mental verb does not take its direct object(s) directly. Instead its direct government is suspended due to the presence of a suspender. This phenomenon is called suspension of government. Let’s take a look at an example. Consider a sentence where ظَنَّ exercises it governing effect.

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا قَائِمًا « I thought Zayd to be standing. »

Now let’s see what happens when the original subject (زَيْد) and info (قَائِم) are preceded by a لَ of beginning. The sentence becomes:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ لَزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ [Howell §445 2/158]
    « I thought, assuredly Zayd is standing. »

As you can see, the original subject (زَيْد) and info (قَائِم) are now both in the u-case. This is because the effect of the لَ of beginning is to cause a subject and info to be in the u-case. The government of ظَنَّ is thus suspended. The inner sentence لَزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ is said to be in the positional a-case as a direct object of ظَنَّ that fulfils the role of two direct objects.

The proof that the inner sentence لَزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ is in the positional a-case and that the government of ظَنَّ is suspended, is that if we were to put a conjunction after this sentence, then the governing effect of ظَنَّ would resume. For example:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ لَزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ وَعَمْرًا مُنْطَلِقًا [Howell §445 2/158]
    « I thought, assuredly Zayd is standing and Ɛamr is setting out. »

The suspenders that suspend the government of the mental verbs are discussed in the subsections below:

33.10.1 The negaters مَا, إِنْ, and لَا

Examples:

  1. لَقَدۡ عَلِمۡتَ مَا هَـٰۤؤُلَاۤءِ یَنطِقُونَ [سورة الأنبياء 21:65 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/346; Howell §445 2/160]
    « “You have already known that these do not speak!” »

In example () above, the negater مَا prevents عَلِمْتَ from directly governing two direct objects. Instead, the sentence مَا هَؤُلَاءِ يَنْطِقُونَ is in the positional a-case as a direct object that fulfils the role two direct objects. Similarly:

  1. عَلِمْتُ وَاللهِ إِنْ زَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ [Howell §445 2/160]
    « I knew, by Allāh, Zayd is not standing. »

  2. یَوۡمَ یَدۡعُوكُمۡ فَتَسۡتَجِیبُونَ بِحَمۡدِهِۦ وَتَظُنُّونَ إِن لَّبِثۡتُمۡ إِلَّا قَلِیلࣰا [سورة الإسراء 17:52 cited by Howell §445 2/160]
    « On the Day He will call you and you will respond with praise of Him and think that you had not remained [in the world] except for a little.” »

  3. عَلِمْتُ وَاللهِ لَا زَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ ولَا عَمْرٌو [Howell §445 2/160]
    « I knew, by Allāh, Zayd is not in the house, nor Ɛamr. »

33.10.2 لَ of beginning

We have already seen in example () above, how لَ of beginning suspends the direct government of a mental verb. Here is another example:

  1. وَلَقَدۡ عَلِمُوا۟ لَمَنِ ٱشۡتَرَىٰهُ مَا لَهُۥ فِی ٱلۡـَٔاخِرَةِ مِنۡ خَلَـٰقࣲۚ [سورة البقرة 2:102 cited by Howell §445 2/160]
    « But they [i.e., the Children of Israel] certainly knew that whoever purchased it [i.e., magic] would not have in the Hereafter any share. »

33.10.3 لَ of oath

  1. لَقدْ عَلِمْتُ لَتَأْتِيَنَّ مَنِيَّتِي ~ إِنَّ الْمَنَايًا لَا تَطِيشُ سِهَامُهَا [النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/346; Howell §445 2/158]
    « And assuredly, I have known (by God) verily my fate will come. Verily the fates—their arrows do not miss. »

The presence of the لَ of oath in لَتَأْتِيَنَّ suspends the direct government of عَلِمْتُ. Otherwise the sentence would have been:

  1. عَلِمْتُ مَنِيَّتِي آتِيَةً [النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/436]
    « I have known my fate to be coming. »

33.10.4 A question sentence

A question sentence can follow a mental verb and in such a case the government of the mental verb is suspended if the entire question sentence is its direct object.

Here is an example:

  1. عَلِمْتُ أَزَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ أَمْ عَمْرٌو [Howell §445 2/159]
    « I knew whether Zayd was in the house or Ɛamr. »

In example () above, the entire question sentence أَزَيْدٌ فِي الدَّارِ أَمْ عَمْرٌو is in the a-case and it fulfils the role the two direct objects of عَلِمْتُ. That is the traditional explanation. However, it is also possible that عَلِمَ here requires only one direct object. In which case the question sentence fulfils the role the one required direct object.

Another point to note is that the question sentence is now no longer a question to the listener. That is why we have not puntuated the translation with a question mark (?). Rather, the entire example sentence is signifying that the speaker knows the answer to the question.

Here is another example:

  1. وَلَتَعۡلَمُنَّ أَیُّنَاۤ أَشَدُّ عَذَابࣰا وَأَبۡقَىٰ [سورة طه 20:71 cited by Howell §445 2/159]
    « and you will surely know which of us is more severe in [giving] punishment and more enduring »

In example () above, there are three possible interpretations33:

  1. The first interpretation, and the one relevant to our discussion here, is that أَيّ is a subject and أَشَدّ is its info. The question sentence أَیُّنَا أَشَدُّ عَذَابًا وَأَبْقَىٰ fulfils the role the two direct objects of تَعْلَمُنَّ.
  2. Another interpretation is تَعْلَمُنَّ here only requires one direct object and the question sentence is that (positional) direct object.
  3. A third interpretation is that أَيّ is a connected noun. أَيّ itself (not the entire question sentence) is the direct object of تَعْلَمُنَّ. And أَيّ is inflexible ending with a d̤͡hammah because of a phenomenon described in Section 39.6.3.

Here are some more examples:

  1. عَلِمْتُ مَتَىٰ السَّفَرُ [Howell §445 2/159]
    « I knew when the journey was (or would be). »

  2. عَلِمْتُ أَبُو مَنْ زَيْدٌ [Howell §445 2/159]
    « I knew whose father was Zayd. »

  3. عَلِمْتُ صَبِيحَةَ أَيِّ يَوْمٍ سَفَرُكَ [Howell §445 2/159]
    « I knew the morning of which day your journey was (or will be). »

  4. وَسَیَعۡلَمُ ٱلَّذِینَ ظَلَمُوۤا۟ أَیَّ مُنقَلَبࣲ یَنقَلِبُونَ [سورة الشعراء 26:227 cited by Howell §445 2/159]
    « And those who have wronged are going to know to what [kind of] return they will be returned. »

In example () above, أَيّ is a question-device in the a-case as a absolute object, governed by يَنْقَلِبُونَ.34 The entire question sentence is in the a-case governed by سَيَعْلَمُ.

33.10.5 Other suspenders of government

There are other, less common, suspenders discussed by the grammarians. We will list some of them below:

لَعَلَّ

Example:

  1. وَإِنۡ أَدۡرِی لَعَلَّهُۥ فِتۡنَةࣱ لَّكُمۡ وَمَتَـٰعٌ إِلَىٰ حِینࣲ [سورة الأنبياء 21:111 cited by Howell §445 2/160]
    « And I know not; perhaps it is a trial for you and enjoyment for a time.” »

The conditional-device لَوْ

For example:

  1. وَلَقَدْ عَلِمَ الْأَقْوَامُ لَوْ أَنَّ حَاتِمًا ~ أَرَادَ ثَرَاءَ الْمَالِ كَانَ لَهُ وَفْرٌ [Howell §445 2/160]
    « And the peoples have known that had Ḥātim desired abundance of property, he would have had affluence. »

33.10.5.1 إِنَّ that has a differentiator لَ

Example:

  1. عَلِمْتُ إِنَّ زَيْدًا لَقَائِمٌ [Howell §445 2/161]
    « I knew: indeed Zayd is standing. »

33.10.6 The verbs whose government may be suspended

Suspension of government is possible for the following categories of verbs:

The flexible mental verbs that take two direct objects

We have seen examples of suspension with mental verbs that take two direct objects like ظَنَّ, عَلِمَ, حَسِبَ, etc. above.

mental verbs that are rigid like هَبْ « grant » and تَعَلَّمْ « learn », which are only used in the verb of command, are excluded from having their government suspended.

The verbs of rendering, like صَيَّرَ « to make x become y » تَرَكَ « to leave x as y », etc., are also excluded.

Any other mental verb even if it does not take two direct objects

Some mental verbs may take only one direct object, like عَرَفَ « to recognize, know ». These too are subject to suspension. So, for example, we can say:

  1. عَرَفْتُ مَنْ أَبُوكَ [Howell §445 2/164]
    « I knew who your father is/was. »

In example () above, عَرَفْتُ would have taken a noun as a direct object, but the question-device suspends its government and so the entire question sentence مَنْ أَبُوكَ fulfils the role of its direct object.

Other mental verbs that take only only one direct object (like عَلِمَ when used to mean « to know »), or that take an indirect direct object (like تَفَكَّرَ فِي « to think about »), are also similarly subject to suspension.35

Verbs which can enable the mental verbs

There are some verbs that are not mental verbs per se, but they can enable the mental verbs. These verbs too may be subject to suspension.36 Let’s explain what we mean with some examples.

The verb رَأَى « to see » enables its doer to know. Therefore, it may be subject to suspension of government. For example:

  1. أَمَا تَرَى أَيُّ بَرْقٍ هَـٰهُنَا [Howell §445 2/165]
    « Do you not see which [kind of] lightning is here? »

Similarly, the verb سَأَلَ « to ask » enables one to know. Therefore its government can be suspended. For example:

  1. اسْأَلْ أَيُّهُمْ زَيْدٌ [البسيط لابن أبي الربيع 446]
    « Ask which of them is Zayd »

  2. یَسۡـَٔلُونَ أَیَّانَ یَوۡمُ ٱلدِّینِ [سورة الذاريات 51:12 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/347]
    « They ask, “When is the Day of Recompense?” »

The same can be said of the verb نَظَرَ « to look ». Example:

  1. فَلۡیَنظُرۡ أَیُّهَاۤ أَزۡكَىٰ طَعَامࣰا [سورة الكهف 18:19 cited by النحو العربي: أحكام ومعان 1/347]
    « and let him look to which is the best of food »

33.11 Neutralization of government

Similar to suspension is is another phenomenon called neutralization. Neutralization occurs when a mental verb exercises no government at all.

Consider the following sentence:

  1. ظَنَنْتُ زَيْدًا عَالِمًا [Howell §444 2/155]
    « I thought Zayd to be knowledgeable. »

In example () above, ظَنَّ is governing the original subject (زَيْد) and original info (عَالِم) as usual.

If we move the position of the verb (as is allowed), then the the original subject (زَيْد) and original info (عَالِم) can remain direct objects. So we can say:

  1. زَيْدًا ظَنَنْتُ عَالِمًا [Howell §444 2/155]
    « Zayd I thought to be knowledgeable. »

  2. زَيْدًا عَالِمً اظَنَنْتُ [Howell §444 2/155]
    « Zayd to be knowledgeable I thought. »

However, when the verb is moved from its initial position to an intervening or final position, then its government may optionally be neutralized. In that case, زَيْد and عَالِم stay as the subject and info, in the u-case. So we can say:

  1. زَيْدٌ ظَنَنْتُ عَالِمٌ « Zayd I thought to be knowledgeable. »

  2. زَيْدٌ عَالِمٌ اظَنَنْتُ [Howell §444 2/156]
    « Zayd to be knowledgeable I thought. »

The difference between neutralization and suspension is that suspension still governs in meaning, if not in wording. It’s just that the suspender introduces a new sentence that then fulfils the role the direct objects of the mental verb. Whereas, in the case of neutralization, there is no government of the mental verb whether in meaning or in wording.

The standard rule is that neutralization is only allowed when the verb is in an intervening or final position, as in examples () and () above.

When the verb is in an intervening position, then both government (example ()) and neutralization (example ()) are equally permissible. And when the verb is in the final position, then neutralization (example ()) is considered better, while government (example ()) is also permissible.37

When the verb is in the initial position, as in example () above, then, as we have already mentioned, the standard rule is that neutralization is disallowed.38 So the verb must govern the the original subject and original info as direct objects.

However, there are instances of a verb occurring in initial position while the the original subject and original info remain in the u-case. These are then explained away using taqdīr. For example:

  1. أَرْجُو وَآمُلُ أَنْ بَدْنُو مَوَدَّتُهَا * وَمَا إِخَالُ لَدَيْنَا مِنْكَ تَنْوِيلُ [Howell §444 2/156]
    « I hope and faintly expect that her love may approach; but I fancy not a bestowal of favor from you to us. »

In example () above تَنْوِيلُ is in the u-case while إِخَالُ precedes it. To reconcile this, a deleted state-of-affairs pronoun is taqdīred as the first direct object of إِخَالُ. The second (positional) direct object is then the following sentence. The complete taqdīr is:

  1. إِخَالُــهُ لَدَيْنَا مِنْكَ تَنْوِيلُ

Similarly:

  1. كَذَاكَ أُدِّبْتُ حَتَّى صَارَ مِنْ خُلُقِي * أَنِّي وَجَدْتُ مِلَاكُ الشِّيمَةِ الْأَدَبُ [Howell §444 2/157]
    « Thus was I trained to good behavior until it became [a part] of my nature, that I perceived the basis of character to be training. »

In example () above, مِلَاكُ are الْأَدَبُ are in the u-case, even though the verb وَجَدْتُ precedes them. To reconcile this, a deleted لَ of beginning is taqdīred, suspending the government of وَجَدْتُ, thus:

  1. وَجَدْتُ لَــمِلَاكُ الشِّيمَةِ الْأَدَبُ

Some grammarians allowed neutralization even when the verb is in the initial position. They then do not require taqdīr to explain the above verses.39
